\begin{abstract}
Small open-weight language models face severe context and capability limits on repository-level software engineering benchmarks: on the Gemma 4 Developer Agent Challenge Small Model Prize leaderboard (197 teams, 2026-10-24 UTC), public resolution rates on 4-bit quantized E2B/E4B models range from 0.0\% to 10.0\% (median 5.0\%), compared to 22.0\%--24.0\% for 31B models. We study State-Filtered Deliberation (SFD), a declarative three-stage \texttt{SequentialAgent} architecture in Google ADK and \texttt{swegemma} (\texttt{TriageAgent} $\to$ \texttt{PatchAgent} $\to$ \texttt{VerifierAgent}) that configures \texttt{include\_contents: "none"} on \texttt{PatchAgent} and \texttt{VerifierAgent} to slice out prior tool observations at stage boundaries. Across $n = 16$ paired tasks on \texttt{gemma-4-e4b-it-qat-w4a16-ct} (64 total runs on 4x NVIDIA L4 GPUs), SFD resets stage-entry prompts from a mean \texttt{TriageAgent} peak of 10922.1 tokens to 1500.9 tokens at \texttt{PatchAgent} entry (-86.3\%) and lowers per-task peak prompt length relative to \texttt{flat\_controlled} on 13/16 tasks (sign test $p = 0.0213$, mean 11291.9 vs. 13550.8 tokens) and relative to \texttt{flat\_starter} on 11/16 tasks (mean 11291.9 vs. 13029.3), without bounding upper-tail peaks (max 16146). End-to-end task resolution is 1/16 (6.2\%, 95\% Wilson CI [1.1\%, 28.3\%]) for \texttt{sfd} and \texttt{flat\_starter}, and 0/16 (0.0\%, CI [0.0\%, 19.4\%]) for \texttt{flat\_controlled} (McNemar $p = 1.0000$).
\end{abstract}
