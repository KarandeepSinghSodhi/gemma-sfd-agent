\begin{abstract}
Autonomous software engineering (SWE) agents built on small open-weight language models (such as 4-bit quantized Gemma 4 E2B and E4B) face tight context and capability constraints when executing multi-turn debugging trajectories. We study State-Filtered Deliberation (SFD), a declarative three-stage \texttt{SequentialAgent} architecture built on Google's Agent Development Kit (ADK) and the \texttt{swegemma} harness that configures \texttt{include\_contents: "none"} on \texttt{PatchAgent} and \texttt{VerifierAgent} to bound stage-entry prompt length. Across $n = 16$ benchmark tasks on \texttt{gemma-4-e4b-it-qat-w4a16-ct}, SFD resolves 1/16 tasks (6.2\%, 95\% Wilson CI [1.1\%, 28.3\%]) compared to 1/16 (6.2\%) for \texttt{flat\_starter} (McNemar $p = 1.0000$) and 0/16 (0.0\%) for \texttt{flat\_controlled} (McNemar $p = 1.0000$), while achieving a mean single-call peak prompt length of 11291.9 tokens (vs. 13029.3 for \texttt{flat\_starter} and 13550.8 for \texttt{flat\_controlled}) and resetting stage-entry prompt tokens from a mean triage peak of 10922.1 tokens down to 1500.9 tokens at \texttt{PatchAgent} entry and 1470.2 tokens at \texttt{VerifierAgent} entry.
\end{abstract}
