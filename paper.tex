\begin{abstract}
Autonomous software engineering (SWE) agents built on small open-weight language models (such as 4-bit quantized Gemma 4 E2B and E4B) face severe operational constraints when executing multi-turn debugging trajectories. We study State-Filtered Deliberation (SFD), a declarative three-stage \texttt{SequentialAgent} architecture built on Google's Agent Development Kit (ADK) and the \texttt{swegemma} harness that configures \texttt{include\_contents: "none"} on \texttt{PatchAgent} and \texttt{VerifierAgent}. Across a deterministic stratified subset of $n = 8$ benchmark tasks on \texttt{gemma-4-e4b-it-qat-w4a16-ct}, SFD resolves 1/8 tasks (12.5\%, 95\% Wilson CI [2.2\%, 47.1\%]) compared to 0/8 (0.0\%) for \texttt{flat\_starter} (McNemar $p = 1.0000$) and 0/8 (0.0\%) for \texttt{flat\_controlled} (McNemar $p = 1.0000$), while achieving a mean single-call peak prompt length of 9298.1 tokens (vs. 12855.2 for \texttt{flat\_starter} and 12456.4 for \texttt{flat\_controlled}) and resetting stage-entry prompt tokens from a mean triage peak of 8558.5 tokens down to 1534.9 tokens at \texttt{PatchAgent} entry and 1559.4 tokens at \texttt{VerifierAgent} entry.
\end{abstract}
